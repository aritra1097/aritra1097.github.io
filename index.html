Aritra Basu

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at the University of Massachusetts Amherst. I expect to complete my degree in summer 2027.

I work at the intersection of identity and labor economics. I am an empirical researcher who studies how persistent historical and institutional inequalities shape the way communities and labor markets respond to economic shocks and other social crises. My research also examines paid and unpaid care, with a focus on how gender shapes the organization and valuation of work in households and labor markets.

My job market paper examines whether the historical transformation of American communities during the Great Migration shaped the mortality consequences of the rise in Chinese import competition in the early 2000s.

In my other dissertation essays, I study religious and caste-based conflicts in India and their implications. In addition to my work on race and caste, examining care labor is a close component of my research agenda.

I am on the 2026–2027 job market.
